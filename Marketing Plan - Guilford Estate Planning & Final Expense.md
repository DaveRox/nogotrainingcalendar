## THE TWO ENGINES

Your practice runs on two separate marketing engines that serve different purposes but feed the same revenue stream.

**Engine 1: Final Expense** — the volume machine. Steady, predictable, fast-closing, smaller premiums. Goal: 8-10 paid policies per month. This is your pension fuel and your Council case count builder.

**Engine 2: Estate Planning** — the big swing machine. Intermittent, relationship-driven, larger premiums. Goal: 2-3 cases per quarter, maybe more in year 2 and 3 as referrals compound. This is where the large Council Credit jumps come from.

**The bridge between them:** Every estate planning client has parents, in-laws, or siblings who may need final expense coverage. Every final expense client has a family that may have estate planning needs. Cross-asking is free and takes thirty seconds. Over three years, it compounds.

## ENGINE 1: FINAL EXPENSE

### Lead Source 1: Direct Mail (Primary)

Direct mail is the proven workhorse of final expense insurance. It works because it targets exactly the demographic that buys this product (50-85, middle income, homeowners) and reaches them in a medium they actually read.

**How it works:**
You mail a simple, professional direct mail piece to a targeted list of homeowners aged 50-85 in the New Orleans metro area and surrounding parishes (Jefferson, St. Tammany, Orleans, St. Bernard, Plaquemines, Tangipahoa). The mailer offers information about final expense life insurance with a response mechanism (reply card, phone number, or QR code to a landing page).

**The math:**
- Target mailing volume: 3,000-4,000 pieces per month
- Cost per piece (design, print, postage): $0.50-$0.65
- Monthly cost: $1,500-$2,600
- Expected response rate: 0.5-1.5% (1% is a reasonable planning assumption)
- Leads per month at 1%: 30-40
- Close rate for an experienced agent working warm leads: 20-25%
- Policies per month: 6-10

At the low end (3,000 mailers, 0.75% response, 20% close), you get 4-5 policies. At the high end (4,000 mailers, 1.25% response, 25% close), you get 12-13. The range brackets your target.

**Budget allocation:** Start with $1,500/month (3,000 pieces) and scale based on response rates. You can adjust the list, the mailer design, and the volume after the first 90 days once you know your actual response and close rates.

**List sources:**
- NYL may have approved list vendors through the marketing department. Check with your General Office first.
- Commercial list brokers (e.g., InfoUSA, Melissa Data, AccuData) can provide age, income, and homeowner-filtered lists for the target zip codes.
- MedicareSupplement lead lists can also work well, as people shopping for Med Supp are often in the final expense mindset.

**Compliance note:** All direct mail must go through NYL's compliance review process. Build in 4-6 weeks for approval of your first mailer. This is one of the things you can prep during your runway period.

### Lead Source 2: Facebook/Meta Ads (Supplementary)

Facebook is effective for final expense because Meta's targeting lets you reach exactly the right demographic by age, geography, and income range. It's faster to launch than direct mail, easier to test and optimize, and generally produces leads at a lower cost per lead, though lead quality tends to be lower than direct mail.

**How it works:**
Run simple image or video ads targeted at ages 50-85 in the New Orleans metro and surrounding parishes. The ad offers a free information guide on final expense insurance or a "find out if you qualify" message. Leads fill out a short form with name, phone, email, and age. You call them within hours, not days.

**The math:**
- Cost per lead (CPM-based): $15-$40, plan for $30
- Monthly budget: $500-$800 to start
- Leads per month at $30/lead and $600 budget: 20
- Close rate (lower quality than direct mail): 10-15%
- Policies per month: 2-3

This won't be your primary driver, but it adds 2-3 policies per month for a fraction of the direct mail budget. Over a year, that's 24-36 policies, which matters for both income and Council case count.

**Budget allocation:** $500-$800/month. Start small, test creative, scale what works.

**Compliance note:** All digital ads and landing pages must go through NYL compliance. Facebook's financial services ad policies also apply. Prep during runway period.

### Lead Source 3: Community and Church Networks (Free, High Quality)

New Orleans has one of the strongest church and community networks in the country, particularly in the African American community where final expense insurance has deep cultural roots. This is free, high-quality lead generation that leverages your existing reputation.

**How it works:**
You're not running ads. You're having conversations. You reach out to ministers, community leaders, senior center directors, and fraternity/sorority chapter leaders you know (or can get introduced to) and offer to do a 20-minute presentation on "making sure your family isn't burdened with your final expenses." Churches, senior centers, and community organizations are always looking for free educational content for their members. You give real value, answer questions, and collect contact information from anyone who wants to follow up.

**The math:**
- Presentations per month: 2-4
- Audience size: 15-50 people
- Leads per presentation (people who request follow-up): 3-8
- Close rate (warm, in-person, pre-qualified): 30-40%
- Policies per month from this channel: 3-8

This channel costs nothing but your time, and it plays directly to your strength as a speaker and teacher. It also builds goodwill and referral networks that compound over the three years.

**Implementation:**
- Build a one-page "speaker bio" and a menu of 3-4 talk topics (final expense planning, long-term care planning, estate planning basics, "what happens to your family if you don't plan")
- Reach out to your existing contacts first (churches you attend or have relationships with, organizations you've spoken to through NOEPC or business networks)
- Offer to present at no cost to the organization
- Bring a sign-up sheet, not a sales pitch. Follow up with everyone who asks for more information.

### Lead Source 4: Funeral Home Partnerships

Funeral homes are natural referral partners for final expense insurance. Families come to them in crisis, but forward-thinking funeral homes also offer pre-planning services. A funeral home that refers clients to you for final expense funding is doing the family a service and doing you a favor.

**How it works:**
Identify 5-10 funeral homes in the New Orleans metro. Call or visit the owners/managers. Introduce yourself as a local New York Life agent who specializes in final expense planning. Ask if they have families who need funding solutions for pre-planned arrangements. You're not asking them to sell insurance; you're asking them to refer families who need what you do.

**The math:**
- Funeral home partners: 5-10
- Referrals per partner per month: 1-3
- Close rate on referred leads: 30-40%
- Policies per month from this channel: 2-5

This is a relationship play. It takes a few meetings to establish trust, but once a funeral home knows you and trusts you, the referrals are steady and high-quality. This is a runway-period activity: start making introductions in early 2027 so the relationships are warm by July 1.

## ENGINE 2: ESTATE PLANNING

### Lead Source 1: Educational Seminars (Primary)

You teach for a living. This is your unfair advantage. Most agents trying to build an estate planning practice are not comfortable speakers, and it shows. You are, and that difference is worth real money.

**How it works:**
Host quarterly (or bi-monthly) seminars on estate planning topics for the public. These are not sales presentations; they are educational sessions that happen to be delivered by someone who sells the products that solve the problems being discussed. Topics:

1. "Will vs. Trust: Which One Actually Protects Your Family?"
2. "Estate Planning in Louisiana: What's Different About Forced Heirs"
3. "Long-Term Care and Your Estate: How One Health Event Can Undo a Lifetime of Saving"
4. "Legacy Planning: How to Transfer Wealth Without Transferring Chaos"
5. "Business Succession: What Happens to Your Company When You're Gone"

Louisiana is a civil law state with forced heirship rules that differ from every other state in the country. That's not a bug; it's a marketing opportunity. Most people in Louisiana don't understand forced heirs, usufruct, or how Louisiana succession works differently from what they see on TV. You do. That knowledge gap is your seminar hook.

**Venue options:**
- Public libraries (free, accessible, professional)
- Community centers and senior centers
- Hotels or restaurants with private meeting rooms (small cost, more upscale feel)
- Partner with a local estate planning attorney's office (co-hosted, shared mailing list)
- University extension programs (Loyola, Tulane, UNO, Delgado)

**The math:**
- Seminars per year: 4-6
- Attendees per seminar: 20-40
- Appointments set: 30-50% of attendees
- Close rate on appointments: 25-35%
- Estate planning cases per year: 6-14

Even at the low end (4 seminars, 20 attendees, 30% appointment rate, 25% close rate), that's 6 cases. At the high end, 14 cases. Your target is 2-3 per quarter (8-12 per year). Seminars alone can deliver that.

**Budget allocation:** $200-$500 per seminar for room rental, light refreshments, and printed materials. Total annual cost: $1,000-$3,000. This is the highest-ROI activity in the entire plan.

**Promotion:**
- Promote through your email list (build during runway period)
- Post on your website and social media
- Partner with organizations that have their own mailing lists (libraries, senior centers, chambers of commerce)
- Facebook event ads targeted at 55+ in New Orleans metro ($100-$200 per event)
- Word of mouth through NOEPC and business networks

### Lead Source 2: Professional Referral Network (Highest Quality)

The New Orleans Estate Planning Council is your single most valuable referral asset. The members (estate planning attorneys, CPAs, trust officers, financial planners) are exactly the people who encounter your ideal clients in the course of their work. An attorney who just drafted a trust for a client needs that client to fund it with life insurance. A CPA who just did tax planning for a business owner needs that owner to have a succession plan. A trust officer managing a family's assets needs the family to have proper protection in place.

**How it works:**
Systematic one-on-one meetings with NOEPC members and other professionals. Not group events. One person, one table, one conversation. The goal is not to sell them anything. The goal is to establish yourself as the person they think of when their client says "I need life insurance" or "I need to fund this trust" or "What about long-term care?"

**The process:**
1. Identify 15-20 NOEPC members you want to build referral relationships with (attorneys, CPAs, trust officers who work with the client profile you want)
2. Set up coffee or lunch with each one over the first 3-6 months after launch
3. In the meeting: learn about their practice, their ideal client, and what frustrates them about working with financial professionals. Ask what they'd want in a referral partner. Tell them what you do and who you serve. No pitch. Just a real conversation.
4. Follow up with a thank-you note and a specific offer: "When your clients need life insurance to fund a trust, long-term care planning, or estate planning with whole life, I'm your person. I'll treat your client well and I'll keep you in the loop."
5. Stay in touch quarterly. Refer business to them when you can. The reciprocity principle is powerful.

**The math:**
- Active referral relationships: 10-15
- Referrals per relationship per year: 2-5
- Close rate on professional referrals: 40-60%
- Estate planning cases per year from this channel: 8-30

Even at the low end (10 relationships, 2 referrals each, 40% close rate), that's 8 cases. At the high end, it's 30. This is the highest-quality lead source in the entire plan, and it costs nothing but time and coffee.

**This is a runway activity.** You can start having these conversations now, as a fellow NOEPC member, without any conflict with your management role. You're not selling anything. You're building relationships. By July 1, 2027, you'll have a warm network of professionals who already know what you do and have already agreed to refer to you.

## THE WEEKLY RHYTHM (ACTIVE PERIOD)

You're working 2-3 days a week. Here's how to structure those days so the machine runs without wasting time.

**Assuming 3 active days (the maximum):**

| Day | Morning | Afternoon |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | Final expense appointments (direct mail and Facebook leads) | Final expense appointments / follow-up calls |
| Day 2 | Estate planning appointments (seminar and referral leads) | Professional relationship calls / NOEPC follow-up / content writing |
| Day 3 | Final expense appointments / church & community presentations | Case prep / compliance / admin / lead pipeline review |

**Assuming 2 active days:**

| Day | Morning | Afternoon |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | Final expense appointments (all lead sources) | Follow-up calls / lead pipeline |
| Day 2 | Estate planning appointments | Professional calls / admin / content |

The principle: final expense gets the most appointment time because it's your volume engine. Estate planning appointments are fewer but higher-value, so they get dedicated blocks. Relationship-building and admin happen in the margins, not during prime selling time.

**Lead follow-up rules:**
- Direct mail and Facebook leads: call within 24 hours of receipt. Speed kills in final expense. The agent who calls first usually closes.
- Seminar and referral leads: call within 48 hours. These are warmer, but don't let them cool.
- Never let a lead sit more than 48 hours. If you can't call, have an automated text or email go out acknowledging the inquiry.

## WHAT COULD GO WRONG (AND WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT)

**Direct mail response rates are lower than expected.** This happens. Response rates vary by list quality, mailer design, seasonality, and market saturation. Solution: test multiple mailer designs and list segments in the first 90 days. Kill what doesn't work and double down on what does. If direct mail consistently underperforms, shift budget to Facebook ads and community presentations, which have lower cost-per-lead.

**Facebook lead quality is poor.** Common problem. Facebook final expense leads can be tire-kickers. Solution: build a tight qualification script for the first call. Don't waste appointment time on people who can't afford the premium, aren't in the age range, or aren't serious. A 2-minute phone screen saves an hour of driving to a no-show.

**Seminars don't produce appointments.** Usually a promotion problem, not a presentation problem. Solution: make sure you're promoting to the right audience (55+, homeowners, New Orleans metro). Use Facebook event ads. Partner with organizations that have their own mailing lists. Offer something specific (a free estate planning checklist, a "Louisiana forced heirship guide") to increase sign-ups.

**Referral partners don't refer.** This happens when the relationship is one-sided. Solution: refer business TO them. Send your clients to their CPA, their attorney, their trust company. Reciprocity drives referrals. Also, follow up quarterly with a personal note, not a form email. Stay top of mind.

**You're burning out at 3 days a week.** Final expense can be emotionally draining; you're talking to people about death. Solution: protect your off days. Do not let admin and follow-up bleed into your days off. If volume exceeds what you can handle in 3 days, hire a part-time assistant to handle scheduling, compliance paperwork, and lead follow-up. The math supports it by year 2.

**A bad lapse rate kills your Case Rate Bonus credits.** The Council rules require a Whole Life first-year lapse rate under 20% (or under 7.5% for the 50-case bonus). Solution: only write policies you're confident will stick. Final expense whole life with level premiums and reasonable face amounts should have low lapse rates if the client can actually afford the payment. Do a budget check with every client before submitting the application.
